Receiving and Acting on Remote Feedback p75

Category

Remote Job Interview

Level

Intermediate

Scenario

You are in a remote job interview for a project manager role. The interviewer asks you to describe a time you received constructive criticism on a remote project and how you handled it.

Textbook

I got feedback on a project online. I listened to my manager. I said 'thank you.' Then I made changes. The project was better.

Textbookcritique

This response is functional but lacks detail, professional vocabulary, and specific examples. It doesn't demonstrate a proactive approach to feedback or the ability to articulate the learning process. It's too simplistic for a professional interview.

Natural

Certainly. In my previous role, I was managing a remote software development project. During a sprint review, my team lead provided feedback on how I organized some of the daily stand-up meetings, suggesting they could be more concise and action-oriented. I immediately acknowledged the feedback, thanked her for the input, and asked for specific examples of what 'more concise' would look like in practice. I then implemented her suggestions, starting with a clearer agenda for each meeting and dedicating specific time slots for updates and action items. This adjustment significantly improved our team's engagement and efficiency in subsequent stand-ups. It was a valuable lesson in adapting communication strategies for a remote environment.

Category

Level

Advanced

Scenario

In an academic speaking test (e.g., IELTS Part 3 or TOEFL Independent Task), you are asked to discuss the multifaceted impact of social media platforms on personal relationships and societal communication patterns in the contemporary world.

Textbook

Social media is big now. It helps us talk to friends. But sometimes it makes us feel bad. People spend too much time on it. It changes how we meet.

Textbookcritique

This response is very basic and lacks the depth, academic tone, and sophisticated vocabulary expected for an advanced IELTS or TOEFL task. It presents generalized observations without exploring complex ideas, providing supporting details, or offering a balanced, analytical perspective on social media's multifaceted impact.

Natural

Social media platforms have undeniably become deeply intertwined with modern communication and relationships, exerting a multifaceted impact. On the one hand, they facilitate instantaneous global connectivity, allowing individuals to maintain ties with distant friends and family, forge new communities based on shared interests, and even mobilize for social causes. This has broadened our social networks and enhanced awareness of diverse perspectives. However, there's also a significant downside. The curated nature of online profiles can foster unrealistic comparisons and anxieties, potentially eroding self-esteem. Furthermore, superficial digital interactions might sometimes displace deeper, face-to-face engagements, leading to feelings of isolation despite being 'connected.' The spread of misinformation and echo chambers also poses a considerable challenge to genuine societal communication. Therefore, while offering unparalleled opportunities for connection, social media demands a critical and balanced approach to safeguard our well-being and the quality of our relationships.

60-Second Daily English Upgrade

Natural Phrase of the Week: Caught up on

This week's phrase helps you talk about getting things done! To "catch up on" something means to do something you haven't had time to do recently, or to learn about recent events or developments after a period of absence or being busy.

Example in conversation:

"I spent Saturday hiking, then on Sunday, I just took it easy and caught up on some reading."

Micro-tip: You can use "catch up on" for various activities: "catch up on sleep," "catch up on emails," "catch up on work," or "catch up on the latest news."
