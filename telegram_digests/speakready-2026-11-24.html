Politely Declining New Tasks or Requesting Help p84

Category

Workplace Communication

Level

Advanced

Scenario

Your manager assigns you a new urgent task, but you are already at capacity with existing projects. How do you professionally communicate your current workload and negotiate priorities or seek assistance?

Textbook

I can't do this. I have too much work. Someone else should do it.

Textbookcritique

This response is overly blunt, unprofessional, and doesn't offer any solutions or demonstrate a willingness to collaborate. It also lacks the respectful tone expected in a professional setting.

Natural

Thanks for thinking of me for this, [Manager's Name]. I understand this new task is urgent. Currently, I'm deep into [Project A] and [Project B], both of which are high-priority with deadlines approaching. Adding this new task right now would put a significant strain on my capacity and potentially impact the quality or timely delivery of my existing commitments. Would you like me to reprioritize, or could we explore if [Colleague's Name] or another team member might have the bandwidth to tackle this in the short term? I'm happy to help strategize if needed.

⚡ 60-Second Daily English Upgrade

🚀 60-Second Daily English Upgrade: Circle Back

Natural Phrase: To circle back (on something)

What it means: To return to a previous topic or discussion, or to revisit a task at a later time. It's a common and polite way to defer a conversation or task without dismissing it.

Example in action:

"Thanks for bringing that up, but I'm currently focused on the Q4 report. Let's circle back on the new project proposal first thing tomorrow morning."

🎯 Micro-Tip: Use "circle back" to manage expectations and demonstrate that you value the topic or task, even if you can't address it immediately. It's perfect for professional settings where you need to politely postpone discussions or follow-ups.
